Lab 07: Exam 02 Review

ImportantDue date

There is nothing to turn in for this lab. This lab will be graded based on attendance only.

You can write your responses on your own paper / tablet and use them to study for the exam.

Introduction

This lab is a review for Exam 02. Each group will be assigned one exercise to work on and present to the class. If you finish early, you are encouraged to work on other exercises.

Exercise 1

Suppose you fit a linear regression model of the form

\[ y_i = \beta_0 + \beta_1x_{i1} + \beta_2x_{i2} + \beta_3x_{i3} + \epsilon_i \]

The Variance Inflation Factors (VIFs) for the predictors are

Predictor VIF
\(x_1\) 4.9

\(x_2\)

\(x_3\)

8.5

12.4

  1. Compute \(R_j^2\) for predictor \(x_3\). Interpret this value.

  2. About how much is the standard error for \(\hat{\beta}_3\) inflated in this model compared to the standard error if \(x_3\) if \(x_3\) was uncorrelated with the other predictors? You can assume the estimated coefficients would not change.

  3. Suppose for \(x_3\), the estimated coefficient is \(\hat{\beta}_3 = 2.4\) and the standard error is \(SE_{\hat{\beta}_3}\) = 1.5. What is the test statistic associated with \(H_0: \beta_3 = 0\)?

  4. Use the responses from (b) and (c) to compute the test statistic if \(x_3\) was uncorrelated with the other predictors. How do the test statistics compare? What does this show about a potential impact of multicollinearity?

Exercise 2

This exercise is adapted from Casella and Berger (2024).

Suppose there are \(n\) observations \((x_1, y_1), \ldots, (x_n, y_n)\), such that the relationship between \(X\) and \(Y\) can be summarized as \[ y_i = \beta x_i^2 + \epsilon_i \hspace{8mm} \epsilon_i \sim N(0,\sigma^2) \]

  1. Find the maximum likelihood estimator of \(\beta\) .

  2. Is the MLE unbiased?

Exercise 3

The goal of this analysis is to understand the relationship between a child’s age (measured in months) and their respiratory rate (measured in breaths per minute). Using data from 618 children ages 15 days to 3 years (36 months) old, we fit a model of the form

\[ \text{respiratory\_rate} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \text{age} + \epsilon \]

  1. We check the model conditions and determine we need to log-transform respiratory_rate to better fit the data. Draw a sketch of EDA and model conditions plots that could have led to this conclusion.
  2. The output for the model using \(\log(\text{respiratory\_rate})\) with 95% confidence intervals is below.
term estimate std.error statistic p.value conf.low conf.high
(Intercept) 3.845 0.013 304.500 0 3.82 3.870
Age -0.019 0.001 -25.839 0 -0.02 -0.018

Interpret the 95% confidence interval for Age in the context of the data.

  1. What is the predicted respiratory rate for a child who is 20 months old?

Exercise 4

The goal of this analysis is to understand the relationship between a child’s age (measured in months) and their respiratory rate (measured in breaths per minute). Using data from 618 children ages 15 days to 3 years (36 months) old, we fit a model of the form

\[ \text{respiratory\_rate} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \text{age} + \epsilon \]

  1. We check the model conditions and determine we need to log-transform \(age\) to better fit the data. Draw a sketch of EDA and model conditions plots that could have led to this conclusion.
  2. The output for the model using \(\log(\text{age})\) with 95% confidence intervals is
term estimate std.error statistic p.value conf.low conf.high
(Intercept) 50.135 0.632 79.330 0 48.893 51.376
log(Age) -5.982 0.263 -22.781 0 -6.498 -5.467

Interpret the 95% confidence interval for the expected change when Age increases by 10%.

  1. What is the predicted respiratory rate for a child who is 20 months old?

Exercise 5

We fit a logistic regression model to predict the odds a hotel booking is canceled (1: canceled, 0: not canceled) based on the following predictors:

  • lead_time: number of days between the booking and the scheduled arrival

  • is_repeated_guest: whether or not the booking’s guest is a repeat customer at the hotel.

The data are from the hotel_bookings data set in the bayesrules (Dogucu et al. 2021) R package. The model output is below:

term estimate std.error statistic p.value
(Intercept) -1.113 0.102 -10.913 0.000
lead_time 0.005 0.001 7.884 0.000
is_repeated_guest -0.300 0.440 -0.680 0.496

  1. Explain why we use logistic regression instead of a linear regression model.

  2. Interpret the coefficient of lead_time in the context of the data.

  3. Suppose there is a booking by a guest who is not a repeat customer that was booked 30 days before the scheduled arrival. Compute the following:

    1. Predicted log-odds
    2. Predicted odds
    3. Predicted probability

Exercise 6

Suppose we use a simple linear regression model to predict a home’s monthly electricity use from a its total area. Consider three homes:

  • Home A: Its area is close to the sample mean, but its electricity use is much higher than the model predicts.

  • Home B: Its area is much larger than that of every other home, but its electricity use is close to the fitted regression line.

  • Home C: Its area is much larger than that of every other home, and its electricity use is far below the fitted regression line.

  1. Which home(s) would you expect to have high leverage? Explain.

  2. Which home(s) would you expect to have large magnitude standardized residuals? Explain.

  3. Which home would you investigate first for potentially having a large Cook’s distance? Explain.

Exercise 7
