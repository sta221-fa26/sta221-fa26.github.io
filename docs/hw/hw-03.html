HW 03: Inference for regression

Physical activity and post-COVID-19 conditions

ImportantDue date

This assignment is due on Thursday, October 1 at 11:59pm. To be considered on time, the following must be done by the due date:

  • Final .qmd and .pdf files pushed to your GitHub repo

Introduction

In this assignment, you will explore mathematical properties of inference for regression and use inference to draw conclusions about factors associated with the daily physical activity among individuals with a post-COVID-19 condition.

Learning goals

In this assignment, you will…

  • explore mathematical properties of linear regression models.
  • use multiple linear regression to model the relationship between three or more variables
  • fit and interpret models with interaction terms.

Exercise 1

Derive \(Var(\hat{\mathbf{y})}\), the variance of the fitted values produced by the ordinary least-squares regression model.

Exercise 2

Consider the weighted least-squares regression model

\[ \mathbf{y} = \mathbf{X}\boldsymbol{\beta} + \boldsymbol{\epsilon} \hspace{10mm} \epsilon \sim N(\mathbf{0}, \sigma^2\mathbf{W}^{-1}) \]

such that \(\mathbf{W}\) is a fixed, diagonal matrix with \(w_i > 0\), the weight for the \(i\)th observation, on the diagonal.

  1. The estimator for the weighted-least squares model is the \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{WLS}\) that minimizes the following objective function:

    \[(\mathbf{y} - \mathbf{X}\boldsymbol{\beta})^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{W}(\mathbf{y} - \mathbf{X}\boldsymbol{\beta})\]

Show that the estimator is \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{WLS} = (\mathbf{X}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{Wy}\). Hint: You can use the fact that \(\mathbf{X}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{X}\) is invertible; you do not need to show this.

  1. Derive \(E(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{WLS})\)

  2. Derive \(Var(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{WLS})\).

Exercise 3

If the columns of the design matrix \(\mathbf{X}\) are linearly dependent, prove that there is no matrix \(\mathbf{C}\) such that \(E[\mathbf{Cy}] = \boldsymbol{\beta}\) for every possible \(\boldsymbol{\beta}\). Note: \(\mathbf{C}\) is a fixed matrix, i.e., a matrix of constants.

Data: Physical activity in post-COVID-19 condition

In the academic article “Physical activity in post-COVID-19 condition: A cross-sectional study”, Kopp et al. (2026) use multiple linear regression to study variability in the amount of daily physical activity among adults experiencing a post-COVID-19 condition (PCC). They define PCC based on the World Health Organization’s definition as a “spectrum of symptoms that persist for three months or longer following a [COVID-19] infection and cannot be explained by an alternative diagnosis.” Common symptoms include fatigue, dyspnea (shortness of breath), concentration difficulties, among others.

The data include the baseline measurements for the 141 adults from the clinical trial who were included in the article’s analysis. This article focuses on understanding the level of the participants’ physical activity based on demographic information and factors related to PCC measured by previously validated survey instruments. Physical activity is measured by the 24-hour Euclidean Norm Minus One (mean 24h ENMO). This measure is derived based on accelerometer data and is a reflection of the overall physical activity across an entire day.

The analysis will focus on the variables below. The definitions are in the codebook provided by the authors.

  • mean_ENMO_per_person: Mean ENMO (mg) value over at least 4 days with at least one weekend day for each participant (measured in milligravity, mg)

  • chron_symp_disease: Physician-diagnosed chronic symptomatic disease (Ja (yes), Nein (no))

  • sex : Sex ( Female or Male)

  • age: Age in years (ranges from 18 to 80)

  • edu_t0: Education

    • Lower secondary: Compulsory school

    • Upper secondary: Vocational training or Baccalaureate

    • Tertiary: Technical college or higher technical college or University

  • sts_reps_t1 :Number of repetitions during the 30-second Sit-to-Stand test

    • Measure of functional capacity

  • longcov_pem_grade_t1 : Post-exertional malaise symptom grade (PEM) measured on Likert scale questionnaire

  • Columns starting with facit_ : 13 Likert scale items from Functional Assessment of Chronic Illness Therapy Fatigue (FACIT-Fatigue) instrument. See the codebook for full details on the questions.

Click here to access the full codebook.

The raw data were provided by the authors and are available in physical-activity-post-covid.csv in the data folder of your repo. You may use the code below or your code from HW 02 Exercise 5 to process the data before modeling.

phys_activity <- phys_activity |> 
  mutate(chron_symp_disease = factor(chron_symp_disease, 
                                     levels = c("Nein", "Ja")),
         education = case_when(
           edu_t0 == "Compulsory school" ~ "Lower secondary",
           edu_t0 %in% c("Vocational training", "Baccalaureate") ~

NoteAnalysis goal

Our goal is to use statistical inference to draw conclusions about the factors associated with mean ENMO.

Exercise 4

Let’s start with the model presented in Table 4 of the article.

  1. Fit the linear regression model using physician-diagnosed chronic symptomatic disease, sex, age, functional capacity, severity of fatigue, severity of PEM, and education to explain variability in mean ENMO. Display the model output to 2 decimal places. The estimated model coefficients should match the results in Table 4 of the article. (Note: The authors do not print the intercept.)

  2. Does the model provide evidence of a difference in the mean ENMO based on education? Briefly explain, referencing any statistics used to make the determination.

  3. One of the conclusions the authors draw from the model is “each additional repetition in the 30-s STS test was associated with higher ENMO values”. Does the model support this conclusion? Briefly explain, referencing any statistics used to make the determination.

Exercise 5

Thus far, we have evaluated whether there is evidence of any association between various factors and mean ENMO. Now, we would like to evaluate whether there is evidence of a practically meaningful association.

(Rowlands et al. 2020)define an increase of 0.8 - 1.0 in mean ENMO to be the “minimum clinically important” change for adults who typically have lower physical activity. We will use 0.8 mg as the lower threshold for clinical importance. Thus, we wish to evaluate whether the difference in the mean ENMO between individuals with chronic symptomatic disease and those without is clinically important.

To do so, we wish to test whether the coefficient for chron_symp_disease is less than -0.8.

  1. Write the null and alternative hypotheses for this test using words and mathematical notation.

  2. Compute the test statistic for this test. Show the code used to compute the test statistic; you may use any relevant output from the model in the previous exercise.

  3. What is the distribution of the test statistic under the null hypothesis? Specify the distribution specifically for this problem.

  4. Compute the p-value and state your conclusion in the context of the data using a threshold of \(\alpha = 0.05\).

Tip

Use the pt() function to “manually” compute the p-value.

Exercise 6

The study excluded individuals with untreated comorbidities. What ethical considerations arise when generalizing these findings to individuals with a post-COVID-19 condition who also have an untreated medical condition?
