HW 02: Multiple linear regression

Physical activity and post-COVID-19 conditions

ImportantDue date

Introduction

In this assignment, you will explore mathematical properties of multiple linear regression and use regression to explore factors related to daily physical activity among individuals with a post-COVID-19 condition.

Learning goals

In this assignment, you will…

  • explore mathematical properties of linear regression models.
  • use multiple linear regression to model the relationship between three or more variables
  • fit and interpret models with interaction terms.

Exercise 1

  1. Show that the following is true for the vector of residuals: \(\mathbf{e} = (\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{H})\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\)

  2. Use the result from part (a) to derive \(E(\mathbf{e})\).

  3. Use the result from part (a) to derive \(Var(\mathbf{e})\).

Exercise 2

Show how \(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\) being normally distributed means that \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\) is normally distributed.

Exercise 3

This exercise is adapted from Montgomery, Peck, and Vining (2021).

Suppose we fit the model \(\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{X}_1\boldsymbol{\beta}_1 + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}\) when the true model is actually given by \(\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{X}_1\boldsymbol{\beta}_1 + \mathbf{X}_2\boldsymbol{\beta}_2 + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}\). Assume \(E(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}) = \mathbf{0}\) for both models.

  1. Find \(E(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_1)\), the expected value of the least-squares estimator \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_1\).

  2. Under what condition does \(E(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_1) = \boldsymbol{\beta}_1\)? What is the relationship between \(\mathbf{X}_1\) and \(\mathbf{X}_2\) under this condition?

Exercise 4

We conduct least-squares regression analysis with certain assumptions underlying the regression model. Consider ordinary least-squares regression model:

\[ \mathbf{y} = \mathbf{X}\boldsymbol{\beta} + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}, \quad \boldsymbol{\epsilon} \sim N(\mathbf{0}, \sigma^2\mathbf{I}) \tag{1}\]

This model relies on four assumptions:

  • Linearity: There is a linear relationship between the response and predictor variables.

  • Constant Variance: The variability about the least squares line is constant for every value of the predictor.

  • Normality: The distribution of the residuals is approximately normal.

  • Independence: The residuals are independent from one another.

For each condition, state the components of Equation 1 that are used to represent it.

Data: Physical activity in post-COVID-19 condition

In the academic article “Physical activity in post-COVID-19 condition: A cross-sectional study”, Kopp et al. (2026) use multiple linear regression to study variability in the amount of daily physical activity among adults experiencing a post-COVID-19 condition (PCC). They define PCC based on the World Health Organization’s definition as a “spectrum of symptoms that persist for three months or longer following a [COVID-19] infection and cannot be explained by an alternative diagnosis.” Common symptoms include fatigue, dyspnea (shortness of breath), concentration difficulties, among others.

The data include the baseline measurements for 141 adults who participated in a clinical trial and are included in the article’s analysis. This article focuses on understanding the level of the participants’ physical activity based on demographic information and factors related to PCC measured by previously validated survey instruments. Physical activity is measured by the 24-hour Euclidean Norm Minus One (mean 24h ENMO). This is derived based on accelerometer data and is a reflection of the overall physical activity across an entire day.

The analysis will focus on the variables below. The definitions are in the codebook provided by the authors.

  • mean_ENMO_per_person: Mean ENMO (mg) value over at least 4 days with at least one weekend day for each participant (measured in milligravity, mg)

  • chron_symp_disease: Physician-diagnosed chronic symptomatic disease (Ja (yes), Nein (no))

  • sex : Sex ( Female or Male)

  • age: Age in years (ranges from 18 to 80)

  • edu_t0: Education

    • Lower secondary: Compulsory school

    • Upper secondary: Vocational training or Baccalaureate

    • Tertiary: Technical college or higher technical college or University

  • sts_reps_t1 :Number of repetitions during the 30-second Sit-to-Stand test

    • Measure of functional capacity

  • longcov_pem_grade_t1 : Post-exertional malaise symptom grade (PEM) measured on Likert scale questionnaire

  • Columns starting with facit_ : 13 Likert scale items from Functional Assessment of Chronic Illness Therapy Fatigue (FACIT-Fatigue) instrument. See the codebook for full details on the questions.

Click here to access the full codebook.

The raw data were provided by the authors and are available in physical-activity-post-covid.csv in the data folder of your repo.

NoteAnalysis goal

Our goal is to reproduce the researcher’s regression analysis on the mean 24h ENMO and extend the analysis by considering a potential interaction effect.

Exercise 5

We would like to reproduce the regression model for mean ENMO shown in Table 4 of the article. In order to do so, we start with data processing to prepare the data for the model.

  1. Conduct the following data processing steps used by the authors to prepare the data for analysis:

    • Refactor chron_symp_disease, so that Nein is the baseline level.

    • The authors use three levels for education in the model. Create a new variable that summarizes edu_t0 into three levels: Lower secondary, Upper secondary, Tertiary. Refer to the data dictionary to see how the authors categorize the levels of edu_t0.

    • The original data contains the data for each of the 13 items on the Functional Assessment of Chronic Illness Therapy Fatigue (FACIT-Fatigue) instrument (Montan et al. 2018). We wish to compute an overall assessment score for the model. To do so, compute the sum for the 13 assessment items. The possible scores for the assessment total are 0 (severe fatigue) to 52 (no fatigue).

  2. Fit the linear regression model using presence of diagnosed chronic symptoms, sex, age, functional capacity, severity of fatigue, severity of PEM, and education to explain variability in mean ENMO. Display the model output to 2 decimal places. The model should match the results in Table 4 of the article. (Note: The authors do not print the intercept.)

Exercise 6

  1. Interpret the coefficient of sts_reps_t1 in the context of the data.

  2. Interpret the coefficient of Upper secondary in the context of the data.

Exercise 7

We wish to expand on the analysis from the article by considering an interaction between the presence of a diagnosed chronic illness and functional capacity.

  1. Create a visualization that can be used to explore this potential interaction effect. Use the visualization to comment on whether there appears to be an interaction between these two variables.

  2. Fit a model using the predictors from Exercise 5 along with this interaction effect. Neatly display the model using 2 decimal places.

  3. Interpret the coefficient for the interaction term.

  4. Interpret the effect of functional capacity for individuals with a diagnosed chronic illness.

Exercise 8

Which model is a better fit for the data - the model in Exercise 5 or the model in Exercise 7? Briefly explain your choice using relevant statistics to support your response.
